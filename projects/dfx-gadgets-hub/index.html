DFX Gadgets Hub 4 min read

Making a high-value checkout feel like a small decision

Role

Product Designer

Client

DFX Gadgets Hub

Platform

Responsive web and mobile

Focus

Purchase and checkout, design system

Timeline

April to August 2023

TL;DR

Background

DFX Gadgets Hub is an e-commerce platform for buying and trading gadgets. I joined as product designer for five months in 2023, working with the company representative, a PM and a front-end developer.

Problem

Checkout was the weak point. The platform needed a purchasing flow that stayed simple while carrying far more payment options than a simple flow usually allows.

Approach

Three rounds of wireframing, prototyping and testing across web and mobile, supported by a design system so the two stayed in step.

Outcome

A 90% satisfaction rate in user testing, a complete web and mobile design set, and the product taken from zero to one.

The problem

Checkout was where the sale was lost

More payment options usually means a heavier flow. It could not here.

Buying a gadget on DFX meant committing real money to a high-value item, often on a phone, often on a patchy connection. The brief was to streamline purchase and checkout while integrating expedited payment options - two goals that normally pull against each other.

Every additional payment method is another decision at the point where a buyer is most likely to leave.

Research

Four people, one recurring complaint

The personas came out of interviews, not assumptions.

I ran supervised, controlled sessions with four participants and turned the feedback into personas. Naomi, the persona that shaped most of the checkout work, wanted one thing above everything else: a payment experience that did not make her stop and think.

That feedback went straight into the high-fidelity prototype rather than into a report.

The solution

Every common payment method, one path through

Cards, Stripe, PayPal, Square - and Buy Now Pay Later.

The flow carries debit and credit cards, Stripe, PayPal and Square, with Buy Now Pay Later added so a buyer can commit to a purchase and defer payment. For a gadget marketplace, where basket values are high and hesitation is expensive, deferral is not a convenience feature. It is the difference between a sale and a saved cart.

The work was to keep that range of options from becoming a wall of choices - detailing, amount selection and payment method each get their own step rather than one crowded screen.

Where it landed

Zero to one, with a design system behind it

In five months I delivered the full web and mobile design set plus a design system, and worked alongside the PM and front-end developer to get the product live. It gained substantial early traction and user interest.

Three rounds of testing took satisfaction to 90%. The design system is the part that outlasted me on the project - it is what kept web and mobile consistent as the surface grew.

The 90% satisfaction figure comes from four participants in supervised testing, not from live product analytics.
