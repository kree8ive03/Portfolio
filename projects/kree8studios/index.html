Kree8Studios 9 min read

Building the infrastructure for creative work in Africa

Role

Founder, Product Designer, Systems Architect

Platform

Responsive web, Next.js 14

Scope

73 screens across 13 zones

Focus

Marketplace, certification, escrow, AI tooling

Timeline

2025 to present

TL;DR

Background

Kree8Studios is a two-sided platform connecting clients with certified creative professionals across seven disciplines in Nigeria and wider Africa. Every Kree8ive is assessed, levelled and maintained to a documented standard before they can receive work.

Problem

Creative freelance work in Nigeria runs on personal trust and informal networks. Clients have no way to verify quality before committing money. Creatives have no protection against non-payment or scope creep. There is no infrastructure.

Approach

I designed the entire platform from first principles - a five-sided system covering the client portal, the Kree8ive portal, the admin panel, public discovery, and an AI tooling layer. Certification, escrow-backed milestone payments, a revision system, a collaboration layer and a learning platform. I also wrote the bylaws, the provider agreement, the subscription model and the full service taxonomy, then built every page as a working HTML prototype.

Outcome

73 screens designed and prototyped. 60+ services mapped across 7 disciplines, each with a minimum certification level. A complete operational platform ready for implementation, including the legal framework, financial model and content strategy that most platforms skip entirely.

The problem

No infrastructure. Just trust.

Creative work in Nigeria is a handshake business. That works until it does not.

The Nigerian creative market is real, large and growing. Lagos has world-class directors, brand strategists, web designers and copywriters. What it does not have is infrastructure. Clients pay upfront and hope. Creatives deliver and chase payment. There is no standard, no verification, and no protection on either side.

The platforms that exist are either global and culturally irrelevant, or local and unregulated. Upwork and Fiverr commoditize creative work through bidding wars. Local platforms carry no quality signal at all. Neither solves the trust problem. They just move it online.

So I set a specific brief: build a platform where quality is verifiable before money moves, and where money is protected until work is approved. Not a marketplace. An infrastructure company.

"Not a marketplace. A standard."

Platform positioning, services page

Several rules in the provider agreement and the Kree8ive portal exist for the same reason - to protect the agency relationship that makes the trust model work.

Platform architecture

Five portals. One system.

Every user type has a distinct portal. The design problem was making them feel like one product.

The platform has five surfaces: the public marketing and discovery layer, the client portal, the Kree8ive portal, the admin panel, and the AI tooling layer. Each has its own navigation and information architecture, but all share one design system, one 252px sidebar shell and one topbar pattern.

The accent colour decision was architectural rather than decorative. Violet is the brand and the client portal. Teal is the collaboration zone - every page and component involving Kree8ive-to-Kree8ive work uses teal, never violet. Gold marks the Studio tier and Master certification. The colours are wayfinding.

The sidebar is 252px on every portal page. The topbar is 64px. The type stack is Sohne, Inter Tight and system-ui, with JetBrains Mono reserved strictly for eyebrows, stage counters, transaction references and IDs - never body text. Decisions made once, then applied across 73 screens.

The certification system

A standard you can explain to a client in one sentence

Every Kree8ive is assessed, levelled and maintained. The client never has to ask whether someone is good enough.

Certification runs to four levels - Associate, Practitioner, Senior, Master - assessed across seven dimensions: technical craft, brief comprehension, strategic thinking, cultural specificity, communication, reliability and values alignment. Strategic thinking and cultural specificity were added after the first spec, because the original five did not capture what makes a creative effective in an African business context specifically.

Assessment is three-stage: a knowledge test at 30%, a brief exercise at 35%, and a portfolio review at 35%. The AI scores and recommends a level. A human reviewer confirms it. The candidate never chooses their own level, which required specific UX work to communicate without feeling punitive.

Certification is granular to the service, not the discipline. A Kree8ive certified Senior in Visual Design might be Practitioner in Brand Guidelines specifically, and that affects which briefs they are matched to. It required a taxonomy of 60+ services across 7 disciplines, each with a minimum certification level, made public-facing so clients can see the standard.

AI recommends, human confirms

AI scores all three assessment stages and outputs a level recommendation. A human reviewer confirms it. AI catches dimensional weaknesses humans miss, humans catch context AI misreads. Neither alone is sufficient.

The candidate never chooses their level

Self-selection produces both imposter syndrome and overconfidence. The assessment determines the level. Removing the tier selection step from the application flow simplified the UX considerably.

Cultural specificity as a seventh dimension

The original five dimensions did not capture what makes a creative effective in an African business context. Adding it as a scored dimension changed what certification means on this platform.

Service-level granularity over discipline-level

A filmmaker can be Senior at documentary and Practitioner at commercial. Matching at discipline level would have been imprecise. Service-level granularity meant redesigning the certification page, but produced a more honest system.

Milestone and payment design

100% upfront. Released per milestone. Non-negotiable.

The payment model had to solve the trust problem on both sides at once.

The payment architecture took the longest to resolve, because the trust failure is bilateral. Clients fear paying for work that never arrives. Creatives fear delivering work they never get paid for. Any model that protects one side fails the other.

The resolution: the client pays 100% of the project fee into escrow before any work begins. The money is not gone, it is held by Kree8Studios. As each milestone is approved by the client, the corresponding percentage releases to the Kree8ive wallet, and available balance can be withdrawn at any time.

Four milestone templates cover every project type in the taxonomy, scaling from single-output work to multi-stage engagements. Admin assigns the template, the client confirms the milestone names, and the structure locks before work begins.

Commercial terms sit behind the platform rather than in front of it. What the interface has to communicate is simpler: what has been approved, what is still in escrow, and what is available to withdraw.

Cancellation scenarios covered by policy

Full escrow rather than a deposit

Holding the entire fee from the start means the money demonstrably exists and is protected on day one. The client is not paying the Kree8ive directly, they are paying Kree8Studios to hold it on their behalf until work is approved.

Admin-set templates rather than client-defined splits

Without structure, milestone splits get distorted in ways that expose one side or the other. Templates assigned by project type produce a fair structure without anyone having to negotiate it.

Cancellation policy covers three scenarios, with terms varying by how far the work has progressed. Approved milestones are always final.

AI tooling layer

Claude as product infrastructure, not a feature

The AI layer does three distinct jobs. None of them are chatbot-shaped.

The Claude API sits in three places. The brief builder is a conversational four-stage flow that replaces every intake form - the client describes their need in free text, the AI identifies the discipline, asks clarifying questions, and synthesises a structured brief ready for admin review. The skill assessment generates discipline-specific questions from intake answers and scores responses across the seven dimensions to recommend a certification level.

The hard part of the brief builder was the trust gap between a conversational interface and a formal output. Clients expect a form, because forms feel official, and a chat window feels casual. The fix was a stage stepper at the top - Describe, Clarify, Review, Quote - which frames the conversation as a structured process. Informal input, formal output.

The assessment interface deliberately uses different vocabulary from the certification system. Public scores return as Developing, Intermediate, Advanced or Expert, never Associate, Practitioner, Senior or Master. Conflating the two would let people self-assess their way into a certification expectation.

Brief builder rather than intake form

Forms produce form-shaped answers. A conversational interface produces better briefs because the input pressure is lower. The stage stepper keeps it from feeling casual.

Band language rather than certification language

Public assessment uses Developing through Expert. The certification portal uses Associate through Master. Mixing them would create a false equivalence between a score and a certification level.

Full scope

What 73 screens actually covers

This was not a feature design project. It was an institution design project.

The screens are a fraction of the work. Alongside the 73-screen prototype, the project produced nine specification documents: two phase PRDs, the subscription and certification spec, the revision system spec, an AI resilience blueprint, an API cost analysis, the milestone and payment structure, the platform bylaws and operations policy, and the provider agreement.

The master design prompts document runs to 7,775 paragraphs. It holds a build prompt for every one of the 73 screens, ten complete user flows, fourteen UX gap resolutions, every component spec, and a translation table between design tool and build.

The project is in implementation. Public marketing pages are built, portal pages are in progress, and the full platform targets a 2026 launch.
